# absorbkv — measured claims

Every number here is regenerated by `report.py` into `results/*.json`:

```bash
PYTHONPATH=src python -m absorbkv.report --documents 60 --out results
```

Thirteen of the twenty-two results are not yet in `report.py` and were produced
by scripts in `experiments/`: `remedy_fidelity`, `knee_fidelity`, `logit_split`,
`pagemax_split`, `recency`, `rope_only`, `temperature`, `sharpness`,
`sharpness_matched`, `query_position`, `shuffle_rotation`, `sink_conditioned`
and `model_attention_layer{0,26}`. They need folding in before this file's opening claim is
true of every row.

Source data is 60 MuSiQue documents (seed 0, validation split) captured from
`deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V2-Lite` and `mistralai/Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2` in
bfloat16, two chunk sizes and two prompt framings, 25 GB. The captures are not in
the repository; `runner.py` reproduces them from a single GPU session.

Contexts run 1379 to 4457 tokens, median 2414, so every per-document `keep`
count varies with the document: 43-139 tokens at c=16, 10-34 at c=64, 2-8 at
c=256. Any single "about 2345 tokens" figure is one document, not the set.

"Predicted" is what was written down **before** the measurement, per the working
agreement. "Reported earlier" is what was stated during analysis and later
corrected; those are kept rather than deleted, with the correction noted.

## The structural finding

Paper §3.6 defines the token budget `t = S/c` to cover **both** steps — unlike
Quest, where it counts only the attention — and splits it evenly: step 2
(estimating which tokens matter) gets `t/2`, step 3 attends `t/2` tokens.

Step-2 traffic is `(S / c^r / page) × summary_width`. Substituting their
`kept = page / c^(1-r)`, the page terms cancel:

```
step 2 = (S/c) × summary_width  =  t × summary_width / stored_per_token
```

So it lands on `t/2` exactly when the summary covers **half** of what a token
stores. On GQA it does — scoring touches keys only, and values are fetched just
for the tokens that survive selection. Their derivation is built around that
identity, and it holds at every compression ratio:

```
GQA   c=64:   step2 0.50t + step3 0.50t = 1.00t   ->  delivers 64.0x   (asked for 64)
MLA   c=64:   step2 1.12t + step3 0.50t = 1.62t   ->  delivers 39.5x
MLA  c=256:   step2 1.11t + step3 0.50t = 1.61t   ->  delivers 159x    (asked for 256)
```

**On MLA the value is derived from the latent being scored against, so no part of
a stored token escapes being summarized, and step 2 alone exceeds the whole
budget.** This is not a suboptimal split — the compression contract is violated.
Asking for 64× delivers about 39×.

The remedy is specific and checkable: step 2 returns to `t/2` if the
head-dimension reduction keeps **0.15** of the latent instead of 0.48 (or 0.24 if
the RoPE coordinates are truncated along with it), or equivalently if the split
factor `r` sends more of the compression to stage one.

This is arithmetic from their own formulas, independent of every measured result
above, and it is the one finding here that is actionable for them.

## The subspace finding

Their step 2 scores pages of keys to rank tokens. On MLA the key splits in
two, and the two halves are not equally useful for ranking:

```
q . k_t  =  q' . c_t   +   q^R . k^R_t
            512 wide       64 wide
```

Measured over 27 layers × 16 heads × 60 documents, the second term's standard
deviation across context tokens is **2.08×** the first's, and the two are close
to independent. Under independence that ratio predicts rank correlations of
0.90 and 0.43 against the full logit; measured, they are **0.904 and 0.523**.
The arithmetic and the measurement agree, so this is a property of the model
and not a bug in the instrument.

Because selection only needs an *ordering*, the consequence is direct. Scoring
tokens on `k^R` alone, no page pooling, captures **0.975** of the best possible
selection's attention mass at c=64. Scoring on the whole 512-wide latent alone
captures **0.070**, against 0.003 for picking at random. Their full pipeline
gets 0.875, which is what `k^R` alone gets once the page max is applied — the
latent contributes nothing to the ranking.

`k^R` is not a positional code: it is `RoPE(W^KR x_t)`, a 64-wide content
projection that is then rotated. Two position-only baselines confirm it is
carrying content — the k most recent tokens capture 0.028 of ideal, the first k
capture 0.632, against `k^R`'s 0.975.

### Was the comparison tilted by the softmax temperature?

Step 5 divides the page scores by `sqrt(width x |kept coords of q| / |q|)`
before step 6 averages the softmax weights across heads. That divisor tracks
the width of whatever is being scored, so each variant ran at a different
temperature -- and because head-averaging is sharpness-dependent (sharp makes
it a head-by-head vote, flat lets the widest-range head dominate), the
comparison was not controlled for it. Measured, against the model's own 8.717:
`k^R` 8.000, theirs 20.2-21.6, latent 22.6. `k^R` sat on the model's
temperature by coincidence of `sqrt(64)`.

Two controls, 60 documents, all 27 layers. **divisor**: every variant divides
by the model's 8.717. **sharpness**: a per-head divisor
`sd(page_scores) / sd(model_logits)`, which makes every variant's softmax
spread equal the model's 2.366 by construction, so only the ordering of scores
can differ.

| c | variant | natural | divisor | sharpness | delta |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | theirs | 0.869 | 0.868 | 0.868 | -0.001 |
| 16 | latent_exact | 0.188 | 0.149 | **0.133** | **-0.055** |
| 16 | rope_page2 | 0.891 | 0.891 | 0.885 | -0.005 |
| 16 | rope_page1 | 0.973 | 0.973 | 0.959 | -0.014 |
| 64 | theirs | 0.875 | 0.882 | 0.882 | +0.007 |
| 64 | latent_exact | 0.070 | 0.070 | **0.049** | **-0.021** |
| 64 | rope_page2 | 0.894 | 0.892 | 0.892 | -0.002 |
| 256 | latent_exact | 0.018 | 0.018 | 0.015 | -0.003 |
| 256 | rope_page2 | 0.925 | 0.925 | 0.925 | +0.000 |

The criticism was correct about the mechanism and wrong about the direction.
The natural temperature was flattering the latent, not penalising it: under the
strictest control the latent falls further, and no `k^R` variant moves by more
than 0.014. The subspace result is not a temperature artifact.

Worth keeping beside it: a common divisor equalizes the divisor, not the
softmax. The latent's page scores have an intrinsically narrow spread and reach
only 0.81 against the model's 2.36 even after matching (20 documents, not 60 --
the spread diagnostic in `sharpness.json` is the one result here run at the
smaller sample), which is why the second control exists. That narrowness is consistent with the page-max hypothesis --
the latent passes through `kv_a_layernorm`, so an elementwise max over three
scale-controlled vectors is nearly the same for every page.

One loose end. `latent_exact`'s best layers reach 0.90, so the latent is not
uniformly useless, and layers 19 and 24 -- the two where the content term
dominates the logit -- are among its best five. But across all 27 layers the
rank correlation between latent-only selection quality and `rho_content` is
only **0.237**, below significance at n=27 (`rho_content` per layer from
`logit_split.json`, latent-only quality from `sharpness_matched.json`). The
sharpest case against the story is layer 5: **first of 27** for latent-only
selection — 0.900 at c=16, 0.910 at c=64, 0.722 at c=256 — and **26th of 27** on
content dominance. The layer-level story does not line up, and I have not
explained why.

### Does it hold away from the last prompt token?

Every other relevance number scores one query token. `q^R` rotates with the
query's position and `q'` does not, so the spread ratio the subspace finding
rests on was measured at a single query-key distance. Two sweeps, because the
captures answer only half of it.

**Stored positions.** The question is appended after the context -- context at
0..2344, query at 2345..2370 -- so every stored query position attends to the
whole context and no masking is needed. It also means they sit within 26 tokens
of each other, so this varies query *content*, not distance.

Read only the first four rows. Past offset 4096 the attention has collapsed

an artifact of one distance.

ratios, while these are pooled over documents and heads. The same data gives
2.00 under the second convention, so the figure is good to about ±0.1
depending on how it is aggregated.

### What this gives RocketKV on MLA

Step 2 reads 64 of each token's 576 stored bytes instead of all 576, which
turns the page size into a free parameter. Traffic in units of `t = S/c`:
`step 2 = (c^(1-r) / page) × 64/576`, `step 3 = 0.500` unchanged.

| c | setting | step 2 | total | delivered | asked | share of ideal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | theirs | 1.064 | 1.564 | 10.2 | 16 | 0.869 |
| 16 | `k^R`, page 2 | 0.262 | 0.762 | **21.0** | 16 | **0.891** |
| 64 | theirs | 1.120 | 1.620 | 39.5 | 64 | 0.875 |
| 64 | `k^R`, page 2 | 0.346 | 0.846 | **75.6** | 64 | **0.894** |
| 256 | theirs | 1.107 | 1.607 | 159 | 256 | 0.731 |
| 256 | `k^R`, page 2 | 0.328 | 0.828 | **309** | 256 | **0.925** |

Better on both axes at all three budgets, and it meets the budget their own
derivation asks for, which theirs does not. Page 1 buys 0.973-0.984 of ideal
but costs 1.03-1.19`t` and misses the budget; page 3 lands at 0.767-0.873 and
0.675-0.731`t`, its worst at c=256. Page 2 is the point that dominates their setting outright.

This supersedes the "keep 0.15 of the latent" remedy, which was the right fix
to the wrong problem: it rationed the latent's coordinates when the latent was
not carrying the signal.

## What was measured and discarded

- Simulations on isotropic deviations — superseded by real data.
- Distant check layer predicting later layers — tests a claim CacheBlend does not
  make; they use gradual filtering and say first-layer-only selection is
  insufficient for deep layers.
- "Deviation mass removed" — invented metric with no job once the analysis
  stopped at KV deviation, as CacheBlend does.
- `W^UK` singular spectra — ρ explains the same failures more directly.

## What was never measured

- **RocketKV stage 1**, the permanent eviction, which does most of the
  compression (43× at c=256) and shares the same forced head-grouping. Untested
  on MLA.
- **End-task quality.** Attention mass is necessary for an unchanged output, not
  sufficient. Measuring it needs their method implemented inside MLA attention
  and a benchmark harness.
- **Large head counts.** V2-Lite has 16 heads; V3 has 128, where one shared
  coordinate set spans eight times as many per-head bases.
- **A real decode state far from the context.** The query-position sweep below
  closes the version of this that the captures can answer. What it cannot: a
  token actually *generated* 1000 steps later differs in content as well as
  position, and the synthetic half holds content fixed by construction. Past
  offset 4096 the attention collapses onto a handful of tokens, so long
  distances cannot be judged from these captures at all.
